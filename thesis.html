Curriculum-based Deep Reinforcement Learning for Home Robot Navigation with Natural Language Interaction

Teaching home robots complex tasks progressively—from simple skills to multi-step human instructions

Thesis PDF Related Paper GitHub

Abstract

Home robots must combine perception, navigation, object interaction, and natural-language understanding to execute everyday human instructions. However, directly training a reinforcement-learning agent on complex multi-step tasks can make exploration and learning difficult.

This research investigates a curriculum-based approach in which a robot progressively acquires foundational skills before attempting complete household instructions. Visual observations and language commands are integrated through multi-modal deep reinforcement learning, and five curriculum strategies are evaluated in the AI2-THOR home simulation environment.

Method

The robot learns an end-to-end policy from what it sees and what a human asks it to do.

Curriculum Learning

Instead of learning the entire instruction at once, the robot can progressively build the skills needed for the final task.

[IMAGES - Simple four-stage illustration: "Find bread" → "Find + Take" → "Find + Take + Go to fridge" → "Find + Take + Go + Place"]

The thesis compares five curriculum designs to study how different task progressions affect learning:

ICL Incrementally adds task complexity
ReCL Builds the task backward from the final goal
RCL Introduces controlled randomness
MCL Masters one complete instruction at a time
CCL Constructs complex tasks from individual skills

Takeaway

How a robot is taught can be as important as the learning algorithm itself. For long, multi-step household tasks, curriculum learning allows a robot to acquire useful foundational behaviors before combining them into increasingly complex instructions. The experiments also show that the design of the curriculum itself matters, and that visual generalization remains an important challenge for future real-world deployment.
