Abstract

Imitation learning policies such as Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT) can achieve strong robot manipulation performance, but they can remain sensitive to small execution errors and distribution shifts. Correcting these failures through additional demonstrations and full-policy retraining is computationally expensive and impractical for real-time deployment.

This research introduces Online Residual Policy Adaptation (ORPA), a lightweight framework that augments a pretrained manipulation policy with a feedback-conditioned module. ORPA predicts residual joint-space corrections at runtime while keeping the underlying policy frozen, enabling immediate and context-dependent adaptation from human feedback.

Method

ORPA keeps the pretrained ACT policy unchanged and learns only the correction needed for the current execution context. Human feedback is encoded and used to refine the predicted action trajectory online.

Feedback-Driven Adaptation

Instead of retraining the entire manipulation policy, ORPA learns lightweight residual corrections from corrective feedback. The magnitude and direction of each correction can change depending on the robot state, visual context, and current phase of the task.

Training ORPA

Corrective training data are generated from successful manipulation demonstrations by introducing controlled perturbations and assigning corresponding feedback labels. This reduces the need to recollect a large corrective demonstration dataset from scratch.

Robot Behavior

ORPA applies corrections only when needed. Feedback such as “too left”, “too right”, or “too high” refines the ACT trajectory without replacing the behavior learned by the base policy.

Takeaway

What if a robot could correct small manipulation failures immediately, without retraining its entire policy? ORPA addresses this problem by preserving the pretrained ACT policy and learning only a lightweight, feedback-conditioned residual correction. This allows human feedback to refine robot actions directly in joint space while maintaining the behavior already learned from demonstrations. The results show that online residual adaptation can improve recovery and robustness in both simulated and real-world manipulation tasks.

🚀 As for future work, ORPA could be extended to richer forms of human feedback, broader manipulation skills, and more general robot policies to support increasingly adaptive and interactive robot behavior.
