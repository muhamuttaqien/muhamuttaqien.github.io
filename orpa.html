Correcting robot manipulation online without retraining the base policy

Abstract

Imitation-learning policies such as Action Chunking with Transformers (ACT) can achieve strong manipulation performance under nominal conditions, but small execution errors and distribution shifts can still cause failures. Correcting these failures through additional demonstrations and full-policy retraining is computationally expensive and unsuitable for real-time adaptation.

This research introduces Online Residual Policy Adaptation (ORPA), a lightweight framework that augments a pretrained manipulation policy with a feedback-conditioned residual module. ORPA predicts corrective joint-space actions at runtime while keeping the underlying ACT policy frozen, enabling immediate and context-dependent adaptation from human feedback.

Why ORPA?

A strong manipulation policy does not always need to be retrained when it fails. Many failures arise from small local execution errors that only require a small corrective adjustment.

Full Retraining Requires additional demonstrations and repeated optimization.
Rule-based IK Uses predefined geometric corrections without sufficient task context.
ORPA Learns context-dependent residual corrections during execution.

Method

ORPA keeps the pretrained ACT policy frozen and learns only the correction required for the current execution context. Human feedback is encoded and used to refine the action trajectory online.

Corrective Data Generation

Collecting large amounts of corrective demonstrations from humans is expensive. ORPA instead creates corrective supervision from successful demonstrations by introducing structured perturbations.

Takeaway

What if a robot could correct a manipulation mistake immediately instead of retraining the entire policy? ORPA treats deployment failures as a residual adaptation problem. The pretrained ACT policy preserves its learned manipulation behavior, while human feedback provides the information needed to make small, context-dependent corrections directly in joint space. This enables improved recovery from execution perturbations with minimal additional computation and without modifying the base policy.

🚀 As for future work, ORPA could be extended toward richer language feedback, contact-aware corrections, broader manipulation skills, and more general robot policies for increasingly adaptive and interactive robot behavior.
